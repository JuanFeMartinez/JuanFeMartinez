## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** This is authored for this specific person, not a reskinned template. The Bodoni-outline chapter numbers, the browser-chrome `.visual-barra` framing every project like a pinned screen reference, the `baraja` card-fan driven by scroll progress, the avatar re-tinted and rotated into the hero mask's negative space, and the `forma`-driven placeholder compositions that map to project type (not generic skeletons) would all visibly break if pasted onto another creative's site. The manifesto's voice ("decidir qué ve la gente, en qué orden y por cuánto tiempo") is the site's own thesis stated in first person. Clear pass.

**Deterministic scan:** The CLI detector found 34 advisory/warning findings (`src` scanned, exit code 2) — mostly font-size and color values that sit outside the newly-written DESIGN.md token set. Reading these against the actual CSS, most are not random drift: they are small, real, pre-existing systemic gaps that DESIGN.md's first extraction pass did not fully capture — three different hardcoded small label sizes (0.75rem / 0.6875rem / 0.625rem) doing the job one token should; a third hero-blob color (magenta, rgba(150,45,125,...) / rgba(160,60,130,...)) and a semantic "available" green (#16a34a) that are real, deliberate colors but never made it into the documented palette; and a few stray border-radius one-offs (2px, 4px, 10px) beside the documented 5/8/12/14 scale. Two layout-transition warnings (transition: padding on .nav-interior and padding-left on .proyectos-indice a) are genuine layout-thrash risks — both should animate transform instead.

**Visual overlays:** Browser injection succeeded (mutation preflight passed, live-server ran on port 8400). The runtime detector surfaced 55 grouped findings, dominated by undersized-ui-text: ~29 occurrences of interactive/informational text at 10px (discipline tags, nav-index metadata, thumbnail captions, video-pieces labels, and the La Curaçao/Dr. Fabricio carousel section names) — sitting below a comfortable 11px floor. Several buried-raster hits (opacity-0 img elements in the carousel, the grain overlay, the hero avatar pre-entrance) read as false positives — they are animation start-states or inactive carousel slides, not broken assets. kicker-above-heading, cream-palette, pulsing-dot, marquee, and radial-halo are the detector correctly describing intentional patterns already documented in DESIGN.md, not flagging defects.

## Overall Impression

The system holds together with real discipline — the token inversion, the rarity rule on the accent color, and the prefers-reduced-motion coverage across every single animated component are the marks of someone who actually finished the craft pass, not just the concept. The single biggest opportunity is not visual polish, it is sequencing: four consecutive projects with no real imagery sit in the exact middle stretch where a recruiter is forming their opinion, and a handful of small, fixable gaps (10px text, a clipped caption, a spinner with no failure state) are the kind of thing that erodes trust in an otherwise very deliberate site.

1. **The forma-driven placeholder system** (VisualProyecto.css's reticula/flujo/columnas/capas) — an editorial placeholder with real semantic intent per project type, not a "coming soon" box. It does exactly what DESIGN.md says it should.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Four consecutive placeholder-art chapters sit in the persuasion-critical middle of the scroll.**
Why it matters: perfil.js orders BASKIAT, La Casa Corallo, Lucky Dip, and FIND FIH back-to-back, all with imagen: null, right after the one project with real video and right before the strongest UX/web closers. For a recruiter deciding whether the character/game art holds up, this is the exact stretch that looks weakest, and it is positioned to be seen first.
Fix: Reorder chapters so real-media projects bracket the placeholder-heavy run, or prioritize getting even one real image into each placeholder project before this goes live.
Suggested command: /impeccable layout

**[P1] Project deck caption is likely clipped by its own container.**
Why it matters: .baraja sets overflow: hidden, but .baraja-carta span (the front card's project-name label) is positioned at bottom: -1.5rem — outside that box. The runtime detector caught this independently (clipped-overflow-container). If confirmed, the one caption meant to identify the front card of the deck is invisible.
Fix: Either move the caption inside the clipping box's bounds, or give .baraja enough bottom padding/margin to keep -1.5rem visible.
Suggested command: /impeccable audit

**[P1] ~29 pieces of informational text render at 10px, below a comfortable reading floor.**
Why it matters: Discipline tags, project-index metadata, thumbnail captions, and section names in the baraja carousels all sit at 0.625rem. This is exactly the text a recruiter skimming fast, or any low-vision visitor, needs to read quickly.
Fix: Consolidate the three ad-hoc small sizes (0.75rem/0.6875rem/0.625rem) into one deliberate label-scale step at or above 11px, and update DESIGN.md's typography tokens to match.
Suggested command: /impeccable typeset

**[P1] Video loading has no error or timeout fallback.**
Why it matters: Cargando in VisualProyecto.jsx spins indefinitely if onCanPlay/onError never fire — reproduced live (chapter 01's spinner persisted past 2s). A slow or failed load in production reads as a broken panel with no recovery path.
Fix: Add a timeout that falls back to the poster image with a manual retry affordance, and an explicit onError state distinct from "still loading."
Suggested command: /impeccable harden

**[P1] Lightbox (lupa) has no focus trap.**
Why it matters: It sets role="dialog" aria-modal="true" and handles Escape, but never moves focus in on open or traps Tab — a keyboard user can tab straight into the page behind the overlay. It is the one accessibility pattern in the site that is only half-built.
Fix: Move focus to the dialog on mount and trap Tab within it until close.
Suggested command: /impeccable audit
